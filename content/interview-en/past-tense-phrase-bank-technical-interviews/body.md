## Goal: sound fluent without improvising grammar under pressure

Interview answers about **past experience** are spoken performance. Native listeners unconsciously track:

- **tense agreement** (you start in the past, you stay in the past until you talk about *now* or *ever since*)  
- **specificity** (jobs, numbers, tools, not “I did many things”)  
- **ownership** (clear *I* + action verb, not only “we” as wallpaper)

This module is a **phrase bank + grammar guardrails** so you can always open a sentence, then drop in *your* facts. Pair it with **STAR** (`behavioral-star-templates`) for structure; use **this page** for English casing.

---

## The three tenses that matter in hiring stories

| Tense | Use when | Interview-safe pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple past** | finished actions at a **specific time** in the past | “In **2023**, I **shipped** …” / “At **company X**, I **owned** …” |
| **Present perfect** | life experience **before now**, time span **unspecified**, relevance **continues** | “I **have worked** with Kotlin **for six years**.” / “I **have migrated** three production stacks.” |
| **Past continuous** | background scene **when something else happened** (short—avoid overuse) | “While we **were rolling out** the feature, metrics **showed** latency spikes.” |

**Rule of thumb for behavioral rounds:** default to **simple past** for the narrative spine (“what happened”); use **present perfect** only for cumulative skill (“what you have done across your career”). Switching randomly reads unsettled.

## Ultra-high-frequency stems (memorize cold)

These are the **Swiss-army openers** native candidates use on autopilot—safe in almost any “tell me about…” prompt.

| Portuguese intuition | Natural English (past-focused) |
| --- | --- |
| *(Eu) trabalhei com…* | I **worked with** **[people/tool/stack]** **on** **[goal]** |
| *(Eu) atuava em…* | I **worked in / on** **[domain]** · I **operated in** **[environment]** |
| *(Eu) liderei equipes de…* | I **led a team of** **[N]** **[roles]** |
| *(Eu) era responsável por…* | I **was responsible for** **[surface]** |
| *(Eu) entreguei…* | I **delivered** **[artifact]** **under** **[constraint]** |
| *(Eu) implementei…* | I **implemented** **[feature/system]** **using** **[tech]** |

Memorize **whole chunks**, not isolated verbs:

- I **worked closely with** **product** **to** **narrow** scope **before** **we committed** **to** **a** deadline.  
- I **operated in** **a** **high-compliance** environment **where** **every** schema change **needed** review.  
- I **led teams of** **up to** **twelve** engineers **across** **two** time zones.  
- I **was embedded in** **the** platform group **while** **still** **contributing** **to** **customer-facing** APIs.

## Sentence stems — scope & employment

Mix subjects (**I / my team / we**) deliberately; lead with **I** when you personally drove something.

| Stem | Example continuation |
| --- | --- |
| I **worked at / for** … | I worked at **a payments startup** for **two years**. |
| I **was a(n)** … **on the** … team | I was a **backend engineer** on the **platform** team. |
| I **spent** … **months/years** … | I spent **eighteen months** rebuilding our ingestion pipeline. |
| I **was responsible for** … | I was responsible for **API reliability** across three services. |
| I **was accountable for** … | I was accountable for **the rollout calendar**, not day-to-day staffing. |
| I **was embedded in** … | I was embedded in **the mobile squad** to unblock contract mismatches. |
| I **reported to** … | I reported to **the engineering manager**, **not** the product director. |
| I **supported** … **as** … | I supported **three squads** as the **on-call lead**. |
| My scope **included** … | My scope included **schemas**, **runbooks**, and **post-mortems**. |

## Sentence stems — leadership & collaboration

| Stem | Nuance |
| --- | --- |
| I **led a team of** … engineers | clear IC-manager hybrid signal |
| I **co-led** … **with** … | shared authority |
| I **mentored** … **junior/mid-level** … | growth narrative |
| I **paired with** … **to** … | hands-on collaboration |
| I **aligned with** … **on** … | stakeholder diplomacy |
| I **coordinated** … **across** … | cross-functional glue |
| I **partnered with product/design** … | product-aware engineer |
| I **represented engineering** **in** … | outward-facing voice |

Examples:

> I **led a team of five** backend engineers **during** our migration off monolith boundaries.

> I **coordinated** releases **across** infrastructure and compliance **without** blocking weekly trains.

## Sentence stems — technical actions (verbs recruiters hear daily)

Use **strong verbs**, past tense:

| Area | Verbs (past form) |
| --- | --- |
| Delivery | shipped · deployed · rolled out · released · sunsetted · phased out |
| Quality | tested · hardened · fuzz-tested · load-tested · gated · linted |
| Reliability | instrumented · monitored · oncalled · triaged · mitigated · pager-driven |
| Architecture | designed · proposed · simplified · modularized · extracted · deprecated |
| Data & infra | migrated · backfilled · replicated · indexed · partitioned · cached |
| Collaboration | documented · RFC’d · reviewed · paired · facilitated · negotiated |

Full sentences:

> I **instrumented** critical paths **so** we **could** prove **where** latency accumulated.

> I **negotiated** scope **with** PM **after** profiling showed **we could not** ship safely **that** quarter.

## Present perfect — short patterns for “career-wide” claims

Use sparingly; **one or two per answer** max.

- I **have built** … **from scratch** …  
- I **have shipped** … **to production** **multiple times**.  
- I **have worked** in **high-traffic** environments.  
- I **have led** initiatives **spanning** backend **and** mobile.  

Avoid stacking three perfect sentences in a row—alternate with simple past.

## Collocations engineers often miss

| Instead of awkward … | Prefer … |
| --- | --- |
| “I made the deployment” | I **handled** the deployment / I **owned** the rollout |
| “I had experience on Kubernetes” | I **have production experience with** Kubernetes |
| “I participated on meetings” | I **participated in** meetings / I **drove** weekly syncs |
| “I was leader” | I **acted as** tech lead / I **was the** interim lead |

## Mini-templates you can memorize verbatim

**Opening a project answer**

> At **[company]**, I **was a** **[role]** **on the** **[team]**. **My mandate was** **[one line]**.

**Ownership sentence**

> **I owned** **[surface]**—meaning **[metrics/processes]** sat **with me**, **not** **only** **shared** ownership.

**Leadership without authority**

> I **did not manage** **headcount**, **but** I **set** the technical bar **by** **[reviews/RFCs/tooling]**.

**Closing with reflection**

> **Looking back**, I **would** tighten **[one thing]** earlier **because** **[lesson].**

## Common L2 slips — quick fixes

- **Forget continuous tense overload:** prefer simple past bullets unless painting scene.  
- **Article gaps:** “**the** migration”, “**a** regression”, “**an** outage”.  
- **Prepositions:** work **on** a service · **in** a squad · **with** stakeholders · **for** **two years**.  
- **False friends:** *actual* in English ≈ “real”, **not** “current” (use **current**, **today**, **nowadays**).

## Active drills (say them out loud)

**Drill A — same facts, three openings**

Pick one real project. Say:

1. “I **worked on** …”  
2. “I **was responsible for** …”  
3. “I **led** … **through** …”

**Drill B — verb upgrade**

Rewrite weak sentences:

| Weak | Your upgrade |
| --- | --- |
| I helped with monitoring | I **instrumented** dashboards **and** **defined** SLO alerts |
| I was in meetings | I **represented engineering** **in** weekly prioritization |

**Drill C — timeline discipline**

Tell the same story **only** in simple past, then **only** add **one** present-perfect sentence for career relevance.
