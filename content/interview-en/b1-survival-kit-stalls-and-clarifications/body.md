## Why this lesson exists

Hiring loops measure **thinking + communication**. At **CEFR B1** you often know the algorithm but English feels **slower than your reasoning**. Good news: interviewers tolerate accent; they penalise **long silence**, **panic**, or **answers that dodge the question**.

This page collects **repair phrases**, **thinking-time phrases**, and **anti-filler discipline**—all short enough to memorize.

---

## Rule of three (under stress)

1. Speak in **chunks** (“First I clarify…”, “Second I brute-force…”).
2. If stuck > **twelve seconds**, say something **truthful aloud** (“I’m choosing between two approaches…”).
3. End each phase with **one next step**: “Then I optimise.” (**Avoid trails that die**)

---

## Replace panic fillers

| Weak sound | Neutral replacement |
| --- | --- |
| “uuuh / hmm” looping | Silence **one breath** · then starter below |
| “Sorry sorry sorry…” | One calm repair: **“Let me correct that—I meant…”** |
| rambling apology about English | Brief once optional: **“I’ll prioritise clarity over speed.”** Then continue technically |

Interviewers forgive English mistakes more than logical silence.

---

## Honest-but-professional “English friction” lines (say once early if needed)

- “English isn't my **first language** — if something sounds unclear please **interrupt me**.”
- “I might pause to **pick the correct word**, but I'll keep reasoning **transparent**.”
- “Feel free to ask me **to repeat** if my explanation was muddy.” (**Signals collaboration**)

Do **not** repeat these every minute—trust was established tone.

---

## Buying thinking time (interviewer appreciates structure)

Ultra-short (**B1**):

- “**Give me five seconds**.”  
- “**Can I summarise** what I understood?”  
- “**Let me organise** two ideas.”  

Slightly fuller:

“I need a brief moment to contrast **breadth‑first versus depth‑first**, then I'll pick.”

Polished (**optional**):

“I'm mentally comparing amortised costs—I’ll articulate both paths before committing.”

---

## Clarification phrases (DSA + behavioural + design)

DSA:

- “**Can we modify** the original array?”  
- “Should I optimise for **worst‑case memory**?”  
- “Do duplicates appear **sorted**?”

Behavioural vague prompt:

“When you say **ownership**, should I emphasise architecture or **delivery**?”

System design fuzzy scale:

“If we **don’t have numbers**, shall I propose **hypothetical** traffic and revise?” (**Methodical**)

---

## When you misunderstand the interviewer

Neutral:

“I want to reply precisely—could you ask that **another way**?”  
“Would you spell the constraint **slowly**, please?” (**Remote audio issues common**)

Shows intent—signals active listening—not incompetence.

---

## Repairing semantic slips (recover fast)

Structural pattern:

 **`Let me rephrase`** + corrected claim + (**optional**) reason

Examples:

“Let me rephrase—I meant **`O(log n)`** lookups in the heap, not linear scans.” (**Quick recovery**)  

“I misspoke—I should have said **`undirected`** graph.**”

Avoid dramatic tone—**steady** wins.

---

## Polite interruption (busy interviewer monologue training)

“That point matters—I'll anchor my design on **`assumption XYZ`** okay?” (**Confirms uptake**)  

“Could I sketch **constraints** verbally before proposing components?” (**Tactical redirect**)

---

## End-of-answer signalling (prevent awkward hanging silence)

“That’s my baseline answer—happy to deepen whichever area you prioritise.”

Simple:

“I’m finished—questions?” (**Direct but acceptable small teams**)

Tone matters—first variant sounds smoother internationally.

---

## Micro-drills (~ ten minutes/day)

Timed thirty seconds rotations:

 Cycle **six** words you find hard to pronounce—slow then normal (**graph**, **queued**, **negligible**, **auxiliary**, **amortised / amortized**, **idempotent**).  

Twenty seconds aloud **repair template** rehearsal without context **five repetitions**:

“Let me correct that—I meant ______.”

Weekly track silence length in recordings (**target reduce blank gaps** progressively).
