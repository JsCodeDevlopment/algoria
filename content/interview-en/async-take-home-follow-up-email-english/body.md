## Why async English matters

Take-home loops judge **engineering decisions** plus **professional communication**.

At **B1** level you do **not** need lyrical prose—you need:

- readable **subject lines** (recruiters scan inboxes quickly)  
- respectful **boundary questions early**  
- submission messages summarising assumptions and residual risks **politely**

---

## Tone principles

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| short paragraphs (about three sentences) | walls of apologies |
| explicit assumptions (**numbered**) | blaming the brief without proposing a fallback |
| link + reproducible README + commit reference | zipped code with no run instructions |

Warm but crisp beats emotional oversharing.

---

## Acknowledgement and scope clarification (early)

**Subject**

`Take-home — acknowledgement + 2 clarification questions (<Your Name>)`

**Skeleton**

Dear <Name>,

Thank you for the exercise. I've read the brief and plan to submit before **<deadline + timezone>**.

Two quick clarifications so I align with expectations:

1. <constraint / validation question>  
2. <scope or dependency question>

If you prefer, I’ll proceed assuming **<your reasonable default>** and I will note that assumption in the README.

Best regards,  
<Your Name>

---

## If instructions are ambiguous (still move forward)

Single sentence you can reuse:

“If I don't hear differently by **<date>**, I'll document assumption **X** and build on that baseline.”

Shows **ownership** rather than indefinite waiting.

## Submission email (hand-in)

**Subject**

`Take-home submission — <Your Name> (<Role / Req ID>)`

**Body template**

Dear <Name>,

Please find my submission below.

**Repo / zip:** <link>  
**Commit / tag:** `<hash-or-tag>`  
**How to run:** see README (“Quickstart”).

**Approach summary (3 bullets)**

- Core design: …  
- Main trade-offs: …  
- If I had more time: …

**Explicit assumptions**

1. …  
2. …

Thank you again for reviewing my work — happy to walk through decisions live.

Best regards,  
<Your Name>

---

## README headings that help reviewers (mirror in email briefly)

Suggested English section titles recruiters recognise:

**Scope & assumptions · How to run tests · Known limitations · What I would improve next**

You can paste a shorter version into the submission email above.

---

## Polite apology for slight delay (**use sparingly, always with a concrete new time**)

“Due to **<specific short reason>** I need until **<new datetime TZ>** — if that breaks your process, tell me immediately and I'll propose an alternative.”

Avoid emotional detail; propose a **new measurable commitment**.

---

## Gentle status follow-up (no nagging tone)

“If useful, confirming I'm still **on track** for **<date>** — submitting **morning UTC−3 / afternoon UTC+1**.”  

Adjust timezone honestly.

Alternatively:

“Submission **ready internally** polishing README—final link **within 24 hours**.”  

Only if truthful.

---

## Post-interview thank-you (**short**)

Dear <Interviewers>,

Thank you for the conversation today — particularly **<one technical detail>** which clarified expectations.

Looking forward to the next step when timing allows.

Best regards,  
<Your Name>

No flattery paragraphs—specificity earns credibility.

---

## Micro-drills (**15 minutes weekly**)

1. Rewrite template emails replacing placeholders with **true past project names** aloud.  

2. Read submission template in **ninety seconds** without rushing—steady pace training.  

3. Record voice note summarising hypothetical take-home—“approach bullets only”—mirror email discipline.
